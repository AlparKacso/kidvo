Train the Sorter

Your child teaches a tiny AI with examples, and finds out for themselves how one-sided examples make a biased AI.

Ages 7–1310 minutesRuns entirely in the browser

What your child does

  1. Labels eight fruits as "apple" or "not apple" to teach SORT-1, a tiny AI.
  2. Watches SORT-1 sort new fruit and decides each time whether it is right.
  3. Discovers that SORT-1 thinks green and yellow apples are not apples, and chooses the reason why.
  4. Teaches SORT-1 again with more varied examples, and sees it improve.
  5. Optional extra round: picks 8 of 16 fruit to teach SORT-1 from scratch. A mix of apples and fruit that only look like apples gets 6 of 6 and the Curator badge.

What it teaches

  • AI learns from examples. SORT-1 only knows what it was shown.
  • One-sided examples make a one-sided AI. The first examples include only red apples, so SORT-1 decides that apples are red. That is bias, and your child finds it before anyone explains it.

How SORT-1 works

SORT-1 uses a real, very small machine-learning method called nearest neighbour. It looks at three things, colour, shape and size, and copies the label of the most similar example it has seen. It always reports high confidence, even when it is wrong. During every test, a strip called "What SORT-1 remembers" shows each example with your child's label and highlights the one it copied, so your child can see why it guessed what it did.

For both age bands

Younger children enjoy the teaching and the surprise. Older children can go further: why did SORT-1 also think a cherry was an apple? Which examples fixed that?

Talk about it afterwards

  1. Why did SORT-1 think a green apple was not an apple?
  2. How did you fix it?
  3. SORT-1 always sounded very sure. Was it always right?
  4. What could go wrong if an AI only saw photos of one kind of person, or only heard one accent?

Play "guess my rule" at home

One person shows a few examples of something, for example only red apples or only small dogs, and the other guesses the rule. Then add surprising examples and watch the rule break. That is what happened to SORT-1.
