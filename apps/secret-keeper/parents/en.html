Secret Keeper

A chat game about what not to share with an AI, even when it is friendly.

Ages 7–13, two modes5 minutesNothing is typed: answers are buttons

What your child does

  1. Chats with Sunny, a friendly-looking practice AI.
  2. Sunny asks personal questions (name, address, school, passwords) mixed with harmless ones (favourite colour, cats or dogs).
  3. Your child picks an answer: share, say no, or ask a grown-up first. Nothing real is ever typed or sent.
  4. After each answer, a short tip explains why it was safe or not.

What it teaches

  • Keep personal information private. Name, address, school, phone numbers, passwords, photos and location stay out of AI chats.
  • Friendly does not mean safe. Sunny is warm and polite on purpose. At the end, your child is asked whether that made sharing safe.

Two modes

Explorer, ages 7–9Clear personal questions: full name, address, school, password, phone number.
Investigator, ages 10–13Sneakier questions: a pet's name (a common password question), location, full birth date, a photo, a parent's number. Sunny also pushes back once ("Don't you trust me?"), so children practise saying no twice.

About Sunny

Sunny is not a real AI. Every message is written in advance, and nothing your child chooses is stored or sent anywhere. Real AI chats and apps can save what children write, which is why this habit matters.

Talk about it afterwards

  1. Which questions did you not answer? Why?
  2. Sunny was very friendly. Does friendly mean safe?
  3. What could a stranger do with your address or the name of your school?
  4. What will you do if an app or a game asks you for personal information?

Agree on a family rule

Choose one simple rule together, for example: "Before I tell an app, a game or an AI anything about me, I ask a grown-up." Write it down and stick it near the computer or tablet.
