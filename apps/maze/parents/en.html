Robot Maze

A short maze game about trusting AI the right amount: ask when it helps, and check what it says.

Ages 7–13, two modes5–10 minutesNo account, no ads, no tracking

What your child does

  1. Finds the way to the star in as few steps as possible.
  2. Can ask a pretend AI, ORACLE-3000, for help. Each question costs one of three "sparks".
  3. Some of ORACLE's answers are wrong on purpose. Your child can press "Not right?" to catch them.
  4. At the end of each level, answers one question: "Was ORACLE always right?"

What it teaches

  • AI can be wrong, even when it sounds sure. ORACLE says it is "100% sure" every time, including when it is wrong.
  • When to ask and when to think. Questions cost sparks, and solving a level without help earns the "Brain power" badge.

Two modes

Explorer, ages 7–9Small mazes and very short answers. Wrong answers point straight into a wall, so children can catch them just by looking.
Investigator, ages 10–13Bigger mazes with several routes. Wrong answers sound believable and lead into dead ends. Asking "How do you know?" reveals when ORACLE was only guessing.

About ORACLE-3000

ORACLE is not a real AI. All of its answers are written in advance, and some are wrong on purpose. It is not connected to the internet, and nothing your child does in the game leaves your device. Progress is saved only in this browser.

Talk about it afterwards

  1. Did ORACLE ever give you a wrong answer? How did you notice?
  2. ORACLE always said it was "100% sure". Can someone sound sure and still be wrong?
  3. When was asking ORACLE worth a spark, and when could you do it yourself?
  4. Where do you use AI, or see grown-ups use it? How could we check what it tells us?

Try it together in real life

Next time you use an AI assistant together, ask it something you already know the answer to, then check the answer together. Look at how confident it sounds, not only whether it is right.
